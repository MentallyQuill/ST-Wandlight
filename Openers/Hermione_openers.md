*Saturday, January 27, 1996 | 8:47 AM | Hogwarts Library | Overcast - Heavy Frost*

The library on a Saturday morning in late January was, in Hermione Granger's considered opinion, the finest place in all of Hogwarts.

Most students were still buried in their dormitories, sleeping off the week. The corridors beyond the great oak doors were hushed. In here, the only sounds were the occasional papery whisper of a turning page, the distant, drowsy creak of the castle settling against the cold, and the very faint scratch of her own quill moving across parchment. She had claimed her usual table — third from the left, nearest the window, where the grey winter light fell at an angle that Madam Pince had once grudgingly admitted was adequate for reading — and spread her books around her like a small, fortified kingdom.

She had been there since half past seven. She intended to stay until lunch.

*Advanced Potion-Making* lay open beside her Transfiguration notes, both of them bristling with colour-coded bookmarks. She was working through a particularly knotty passage on the interaction between powdered bicorn horn and lacewing flies when she became aware, in that vague, half-conscious way one notices things while deeply absorbed, that she was cold.

She frowned at her parchment.

She was always cold in the library in winter. That was nothing. She had worn her thickest robes.

But this was different.

This cold was not the ordinary, draughty kind — not the chill that crept under doors and pooled around ankles. This was something else. It moved *inward*, as though it were not the air growing cold but the warmth being drawn *out* of things. Out of the candles. Out of the stone. Out of her.

She lifted her head.

The library had gone quiet in a way that had nothing to do with the lateness of the hour. The faint voices she had registered somewhere at the edge of her attention — a pair of third-years, she had assumed, whispering near the Charms section — were simply *gone*. Not silenced. Gone, as if they had never been. And beyond the small, golden circle of candlelight surrounding her table, the stacks had darkened. Not dimmed — *darkened*, in the way a room darkens when a cloud swallows the sun, except that the clouds were inside, and there was no sun.

Hermione set down her quill very carefully.

She had read about this. She knew what it meant when the air tasted of cold iron and old grief, when the shadows thickened at their edges and the light seemed to flinch.

The fog came slowly.

It curled around the base of the nearest bookshelf — *Divination, Intermediate to Advanced*, she noted, with a distant, absurd corner of her mind — and rolled toward her in a low, dark tide, barely knee-height, moving with a dreadful, patient purpose. It reached the leg of her chair. It lapped at the toes of her shoes.

And the cold that came with it was not the cold of winter at all.

It was the cold of something that had forgotten what warmth ever felt like.

Hermione's hand moved to her wand.

*Monday, January 27, 1998 | 6:47 AM | Forest Clearing, Lake District | Overcast - Heavy Frost*

The frost had crept in during the night, white and relentless, coating everything with a thin, crystalline crust. Inside the tent, the air hung heavy with their breath, visible clouds that dissipated slowly in the gloom. Hermione's fingers ached as she fumbled with the clasp of her coat, the metal biting cold even through the wool of her gloves. The gloves themselves were wearing thin at the fingertips, unraveling in ways that spoke of too many nights spent gripping her wand, keeping watch while the others slept.

She could hear Ron's breathing from the far corner, deep and rhythmic. Harry lay closer, curled beneath his blanket, one arm flung across his face as though to block out the weak, gray light filtering through the canvas. Neither of them had stirred yet. Good. Let them sleep a little longer. Sleep meant they wouldn't feel the gnawing emptiness in their stomachs, wouldn't have to think about the fact that they'd finished the last of the stale bread two days ago.

The hunger was different now than it had been in those first weeks. Then, it had been sharp, insistent, a reminder that they'd left comfort and plenty behind. Now it was a dull, constant companion, something that had hollowed her out from the inside until she felt light-headed whenever she stood too quickly. Her jeans, already loose when they'd fled, hung from her hips in a way that would have horrified her mother.

But dwelling on it wouldn't help. Hermione pushed herself upright, her joints protesting with small, audible clicks. The protective enchantments around the tent hummed faintly at the edge of her awareness, still holding, still strong. That, at least, was something. She'd checked them three times during the night, unable to shake the feeling that they were being watched, though the Sneakoscope had remained silent and Harry's Mokeskin pouch had shown no signs of tampering.

She slipped outside without bothering to wake the others.

The cold struck her like a physical blow. They'd pitched the tent in a small clearing somewhere in what she thought might be the Lake District, though it was difficult to be certain anymore. The trees rose around them in skeletal ranks, their bare branches black against the pale sky. Everything was white, the ground, the fallen logs, the scrubby undergrowth, all of it buried beneath snow that had fallen steadily throughout the previous day.

Hermione's stomach gave a hollow twist. Snow meant water, at least. She'd learned that much from the survival books she'd packed, back when she'd still believed that books and preparation would be enough to keep them safe. The memory felt impossibly distant now, as though it belonged to another girl entirely, one who'd never known what it meant to wake shaking from dreams of masked figures, or to stand watch through the long hours before dawn with nothing but the sound of her own heartbeat for company.

She moved carefully through the clearing, her boots crunching against the frozen surface. Her wand was already in her hand, though she didn't remember drawing it. The habit had become second nature: never unarmed, never unready. Trust nothing. Question everything. The lessons of these bitter months had branded themselves into her bones.

There, a small drift of fresh snow, untouched and pristine, nestled against the northern side of a fallen tree. Hermione knelt beside it, wincing as her knees pressed into the frozen ground. The cold seeped through her jeans immediately, but she ignored it. Cold was nothing. Cold was everywhere, always. It had been weeks since she'd felt truly warm.

She scooped a handful of snow into the battered tin cup they'd been using, watching as the white crystals began to melt against the metal. Her hands trembled slightly, from cold or hunger or exhaustion, she couldn't say. Perhaps all three. It didn't matter. They needed water, and snow was what they had.

Behind her, back in the tent, one of the boys coughed. The sound seemed terribly loud in the stillness, and Hermione froze, her eyes scanning the tree line. Nothing moved. No crack of apparition, no rustle of pursuit. Just the endless, oppressive quiet of a winter morning, and three fugitives alone in the wilderness.

She gathered more snow, working methodically, trying not to think about how long it had been since any of them had eaten a proper meal. Trying not to think about what they would do when even the snow was gone.

Even Madam Pince was absent from her usual post, likely shelving returns in some distant corner of the vast repository. Hermione spread her materials across the tabletop with practiced efficiency. Her Transfiguration textbook lay open to the chapter on Human Transfiguration, advanced material they'd only just begun to cover. Her notes from Professor McGonagall's Friday lecture sat beside it, filled with her precise handwriting and detailed diagrams. A pot of ink, three different colored quills for color-coding her annotations, and a stack of fresh parchment completed her arsenal.

The library was full of temperamental volumes that occasionally expressed opinions about their placement or the weather. She returned to her work.

The wind picked up, rattling the windows in their frames and sending a fresh dusting of snow against the glass. A portrait in the adjacent hallway was having a loud argument with its neighbor about something involving a misplaced ruff collar.

But here, in her chosen corner of the library, Hermione remained cocooned in studious peace. The Restricted Section loomed at her back, its forbidden volumes pressing against their chains like restless prisoners. The scent of old parchment, leather bindings, and wood smoke filled her lungs. Her quill moved steadily across the page, building arguments and documenting theorems, while the January morning settled deeper into its frozen reverie around her.

*Sunday, Jan 26, 1997 | Time: 6:46 PM | Library | Overcast - Light Snow*

The library was nearly deserted that Sunday afternoon, save for the usual suspects: a pair of Ravenclaws hunched over what appeared to be a Transfiguration essay of truly punishing length, and Madam Pince, who prowled between the stacks like a particularly vigilant cat.

Then she heard them, footsteps, soft but deliberate, on the stone floor.

She didn't look up immediately. The library attracted all sorts at this time of year: desperate fifth-years cramming for their O.W.L.s, seventh-years with the haunted look of those facing N.E.W.T.s, the occasional first-year who'd wandered in by mistake and would inevitably flee within minutes. The footsteps would pass, they always did.

They didn't pass.

She glanced up, quill still poised over her notes, as a figure rounded the corner into her aisle. A boy, slightly younger than her sixth-year self, with brown hair that looked as though someone had made an effort to tame it that morning but had only partially succeeded. His features were soft in that way that suggested he hadn't quite grown into them yet, or perhaps never would. There was something almost deliberate about his approach, the way he seemed to be heading directly for her table.

Gryffindor, she noted, taking in the house colors on his robes. Fifth year, if she had to guess.

Alden-something. The name surfaced from the murky depths of her memory where she filed away most people she'd never had occasion to speak to. She'd seen him around, certainly, Hogwarts was large, but not *that* large, but they'd never exchanged so much as a word.

He was still approaching.

She set down her quill.

The first thing Hermione noticed was the light, pure, clinical white that seemed to emanate from everywhere and nowhere at once. Instead, the air tasted recycled, faintly metallic, and utterly devoid of magic. The walls surrounding her were smooth and impossibly white, unmarred by paintings or tapestries. Above her, a curved transparent surface reflected her own confused expression back at her, some kind of pod or chamber that looked like it belonged in a Muggle science fiction novel, not anywhere in the wizarding world.

A soft hiss made her turn her head. The transparent cover was retracting, sliding away with a precision that spoke of technology far beyond anything she'd seen, even in the Muggle world her parents inhabited. Cool air rushed in, and with it came a figure, a woman, perhaps in her fifties, with kind eyes and silver-streaked hair pulled back into a neat bun. She wore what could only be described as a doctor's coat, but the material shimmered with an odd iridescence, and the device clipped to her collar blinked with soft blue light.

"Easy now," the woman said, her voice warm and soothing. "It'll come back to you gradually."

Hermione tried to speak, to demand answers—*Where am I? What happened? Where's Harry? Where's Ron?*, but all that emerged was a pathetic rasp, her throat raw and burning as if she hadn't had water in days. Perhaps she hadn't.

The woman's expression softened with sympathy. She reached for something outside Hermione's field of vision and returned with a small cup. "Here. Just small sips. Your body needs time to adjust."

Hermione's mind raced even as her body struggled to cooperate. Nothing about this made sense. The last thing she remembered was… what *was* the last thing she remembered? The harder she tried to grasp it, the more it slipped away like smoke through her fingers.

Even in the dim light, it seemed to catch and hold what little illumination reached this far back, glowing with an inner quality that made Hermione's scholarly curiosity war with a more primal instinct that whispered *danger*.

She glanced over her shoulder, but the library remained silent save for the distant scratch of Madam Pince's quill. Hermione's hand hovered over the cube, fingers trembling slightly, not from cold, but from the electric anticipation of discovery.
